You run a business and you know a website could be useful, but you don’t know where to start. What is a domain name? What is hosting? Who does what? If all of this feels unclear, don’t worry: that is how most business owners start out.

Here are the 7 steps of a website project, in order, with what your part is at each one. No jargon, just what you need to run your project successfully.
::

## What Is a Website, Concretely?

Before moving on, remember three elements:

- **The domain name** is your website’s address (for example `yourcompany.dz`).
- **The hosting** is the space that keeps your website available day and night, like a shop that never closes.
- **The website itself** is your storefront: your texts, photos, products, and contact details.

You need all three for your customers to find you online.

## Step 1: Clarify What You Expect from Your Website

Before talking about design, ask yourself a simple question: why do I need a website? To be found by new customers, to reassure those who already know you, to present your products, to receive quote requests?

One sentence is enough: “I want my customers to discover my services and contact me easily.” This goal will guide every decision that follows.

**Your role:** your goal, two or three websites you like, and the names of your main competitors. This is also the time to discuss your budget so that the right solution can be chosen.

## Step 2: Register Your Domain Name

This is your website’s address, and it belongs to you. Choose one that is short, easy to spell, and as close as possible to your company name.

In Algeria, the `.dz` extension requires supporting documents, such as a copy of your commercial register. To understand everything, read our article [Everything You Need to Know About the .dz Domain](/en/blog/tout-savoir-sur-le-domaine-dz).

**Your role:** your company’s documents. The registration can then be handled by your provider.

## Step 3: Choose Where Your Website Will Be Hosted

Hosting keeps your website online at all times. For a professional website, check three things: **security** (the padlock in the address bar), regular **backups**, and **support** that answers when there is a problem.

This is also where you create your professional email addresses, such as `contact@yourcompany.dz`, which are more credible than a free address.

**One piece of advice:** avoid splitting the domain, hosting, and website between several providers. A single point of contact prevents finger-pointing if something goes wrong.

## Step 4: Prepare Your Content

This is the most underestimated step, and the one that delays the most projects. A beautiful empty website is useless: it has to be filled.

Prepare:

- a text presenting your company;
- the list of your products or services, with a short description;
- quality photos (team, achievements, premises);
- your logo;
- your contact details and opening hours.

You do not have all of this? That is fine: many providers can help you structure or write your texts, so ask before you sign.

**Your role:** provide these elements, ideally before the design starts.

## Step 5: Approve the Mockup

Before the website is built, you see what it will look like: this is the mockup. You give your feedback, and adjustments are made.

To keep this step quick:

- review the mockup with your team, then send **all your remarks at once**;
- be specific (“a bigger logo” is better than “I don’t like the top of the page”);
- appoint **one single person** who approves on behalf of the company.

**Your role:** a clear approval of the design.

## Step 6: Test, Then Go Live

Once the website is built, you browse it like a visitor: on a phone and on a computer, reading the texts, clicking the links, trying the contact form. Note what needs to be fixed.

When everything is right, your provider puts the website online. It then becomes accessible to everyone at your address.

**Your role:** your final approval.

## Step 7: Keep Your Website Alive

A website is not finished on the day it goes live. It has to be kept up to date: renewing the domain name every year, applying security updates, adding new content or new photos.

Depending on the chosen solution, you can manage some content yourself, with training, or entrust maintenance to your provider. Ask the question from the start.

After that, you can go further: rank better on Google, publish articles, run online campaigns.

## Summary: Your Role at Each Step

| Step           | What you provide                      | What your provider does               |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| 1. Goals       | Your goal, your examples, your budget | Proposes the right solution           |
| 2. Domain name | Company documents                     | Registers the domain                  |
| 3. Hosting     | Your choice of plan                   | Sets up hosting, security, and emails |
| 4. Content     | Texts, photos, logo                   | Structures and formats them           |
| 5. Mockup      | A clear approval                      | Designs and adjusts the layout        |
| 6. Going live  | Your tests and your approval          | Builds, fixes, and publishes          |
| 7. Follow-up   | Your evolution needs                  | Handles maintenance                   |

## How Long Does It Take?

As a general rule, allow **2 to 4 weeks** for a simple showcase website and **4 to 8 weeks** for a custom project. The duration depends mainly on one factor: how quickly you provide your content and approve each step.

## Conclusion

Creating a website does not require understanding the technology. You need to know where you are going, what you must provide, and what you must decide. With these 7 steps, you know what to expect.

Keep this summary at hand: it will serve as your roadmap in every discussion with your provider.
